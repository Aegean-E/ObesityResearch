# Session 3 — Interventions with human evidence against the surviving mechanisms

**Scope note.** This session evaluates agents by **evidence quality against a named
mechanism**. It does not select agents for anyone, recommend doses, or build a protocol —
that boundary is in [guide.md §0](../guide.md) and is a division of labour, not a judgement
about competence. The output is a register, not advice.

**Targets** (what is actually left standing after sessions 1–2):

| # | Mechanism | Status |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Lymphatic transport capacity / collecting-vessel function | live, best supported |
| M2 | Size-filtered retention of adipokines and cytokines → local inflammation → adipogenesis | live, the current reformulation |
| M3 | Interstitial fibrosis (precedes hypertrophy — lipoedema stage I) | live, newly promoted |
| M4 | Tissue mechanics / YAP-TAZ, permissive for adipogenesis | live, untested |
| M5 | Polyol pathway / aldose reductase / NADPH | live, from guide §2.2 |
| M6 | Fructose → KHK → uric acid | live, from guide §2.2 |
| M7 | AVP / osmolality axis | live, from guide §2.3 |
| — | ~~Lymphatic clearance of lipolysis products~~ | **dead** (session 2) — no intervention sought |

## 1. Predictions, before searching (guide R11)

- **P18** — The best-evidenced lymphoedema drug will be an **anti-inflammatory**, not a
  lymphangiogenic agent, and will have a published randomised human trial. Lymphangiogenic
  therapy (VEGF-C) will be preclinical only.
- **P19** — At least one promising lymphoedema agent will have **failed** a phase 2 trial, and
  the failure will be less publicised than the positive result. I should find it and record
  it with equal weight (R5).
- **P20** — The flavonoid/benzopyrone class (diosmin, rutosides, coumarin) will have a large
  but **methodologically weak** evidence base, mostly in venous insufficiency rather than
  lymphoedema, with a safety signal attached to coumarin specifically.
- **P21** — The agents with the *best* mechanistic fit will have the *worst* availability, and
  the agents that are easily obtained will mostly target M5/M7 rather than M1/M2 — i.e.
  **availability and mechanistic relevance will be inversely related**, which is the honest
  answer to "easily achievable."
- **P22** — Plain **water intake** will have genuine randomised human evidence on the M7 axis
  (copeptin), making it the only zero-cost, zero-risk entry in the register.
- **P23** — Nothing in the register will have been tested against **adiposity** as an outcome.
  Every trial will use limb volume, symptoms, or a biomarker. So the register will answer
  "can this mechanism be moved?" and not "does moving it reduce fat?"

**P23 is the one that matters.** If it holds, the register is a list of mechanism probes, not
of treatments for the thing this project is about, and it must be labelled that way.

### The governing finding

**Not one agent has been tested against adiposity.** Every trial used limb volume, skin
thickness, bioimpedance, symptoms, or a biomarker. The register answers "can the mechanism be
moved?" and not "does moving it change fat." Everything in it is a **mechanism probe**.

### Most informative single result

**Ubenimex.** The field's cleanest mechanistic hypothesis — LTB4 drives lymphatic dysfunction,
so inhibit LTA4H — was properly tested in humans and failed, while the *less* specific agent
sharing its pathway rationale (ketoprofen) succeeded on a surrogate. That ordering should
discipline confidence in the whole class, and it is a live argument against over-reading A1.

### Audit

- **Unresolved, recorded rather than omitted:** a search result referenced a 90-woman Royal
  Marsden lymphoedema trial that would bear on selenium. **I could not verify its intervention
  or result in either direction**, so B1 stands with that gap stated.
- **Open items:** pentoxifylline + vitamin E for post-radiation arm lymphoedema (NCT00022204),
  result not retrieved; benfotiamine, no randomised polyol-pathway trial retrieved.
- **Conflation found in my own register:** B1 mixes two different claims — volume reduction
  and erysipelas prevention. The second may be **mechanism-preserving** (infection destroys

- **Unexpected:** that the *approved* aldose reductase inhibitor exists and is in routine use
  in three countries, while the class failed regulatory approval elsewhere — the availability
  asymmetry is geographic, not scientific.

# Session 3b — filling the register's holes (M3, M4, M6) and closing my own open items

The register as written covers M1, M2, M5, M7 and **leaves M3 (fibrosis), M4 (mechanics) and
M6 (fructose/KHK) empty**. That is a real omission, and M3 matters most: session 2 found
**fibrosis precedes adipocyte hypertrophy** (lipoedema stage I), which makes it arguably the
earliest targetable step rather than a late complication.

## Predictions, before searching (guide R11)

- **P24** — **Pentoxifylline + vitamin E** will have genuine randomised evidence for
  **radiation-induced fibrosis**, making it the best-evidenced M3 combination, and it will have
  been tried in lymphoedema specifically (NCT00022204, my unclosed item).
- **P25** — **Doxycycline** will have randomised evidence in **filarial** lymphoedema with a
  limb-volume benefit, acting through anti-*Wolbachia* and/or MMP inhibition.
- **P26** — **Metformin** will have been tried in lipoedema, at pilot scale at best.
- **P27** — The **KHK inhibitor** (PF-06835919) phase 2 will have reported, showing **liver**
  fat reduction without body-fat change — i.e. hitting M6 without hitting adiposity.
- **P28** — **Spironolactone / MR antagonism** will have adipose and antifibrotic data but
  none in lymphoedema.
- **P29 — the self-challenge.** I generalised in §F that *nothing* has been tested against
  adiposity. **I expect that to be wrong somewhere**, most likely in **lipoedema**, where
  excess fat is the defining feature rather than an end-stage complication, so fat volume is
  the natural endpoint. If so, §F overstated and must be corrected.

**P29 is the one I am actively trying to falsify.** A sweeping negative claim of mine is the
most likely thing in this register to be wrong.

### The catch that matters most

**Metformin.** The paper is titled *"Metformin Eliminates Lymphedema in Mice."* Its own data
report **no significant effect on hindlimb circumference or tail volume** — the volume endpoints
were **null**; inflammation and fibrosis markers were what moved.

This is the clearest instance in the project so far of the rule being load-bearing: the title is
a claim, the figures are the data, and here they contradict each other. **Guide R19 caught it**
(read the preparation and the result, not the summary), one session after that rule was written
from a different error. Had I taken the title, metformin would have entered the register as the
best agent in it — broad mechanism, universally available, cheap.

### The synthesis (§K)

Laid side by side, **every** mechanistically motivated agent that got a properly controlled
trial with a hard endpoint failed or shrank on replication, while **biomarkers moved reliably in
nearly all of them.** Ubenimex, coumarin, pentoxifylline+E: null. Doxycycline: 43.9% in one
trial → "limited" across twelve. Metformin: markers yes, volume no. Ketoprofen is the lone
positive and is the smallest, least replicated study present.

Two readings, and the project has independent reason to prefer the second as *partly* true:
either the mechanisms are wrong, **or the endpoints are** — because **limb volume and
bioimpedance both conflate fat with fluid**, the exact defect that resolved C-02 in session 2.
An agent removing adipose while fluid rose would read null in every trial in that table.

That does not rescue the mechanisms. It means **the negative record is less informative than it
looks**, and it raises the value of the one missing measurement — fat segmented from fluid by
imaging — above the value of any further agent search.
