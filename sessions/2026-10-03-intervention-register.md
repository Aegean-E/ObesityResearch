## 1. Predictions, before searching (guide R11)

- **P18** — The best-evidenced lymphoedema drug will be an **anti-inflammatory**, not a
  lymphangiogenic agent, and will have a published randomised human trial. Lymphangiogenic
  therapy (VEGF-C) will be preclinical only.
- **P19** — At least one promising lymphoedema agent will have **failed** a phase 2 trial, and
  the failure will be less publicised than the positive result. I should find it and record
  it with equal weight (R5).
- **P20** — The flavonoid/benzopyrone class (diosmin, rutosides, coumarin) will have a large
  but **methodologically weak** evidence base, mostly in venous insufficiency rather than
  lymphoedema, with a safety signal attached to coumarin specifically.
- **P21** — The agents with the *best* mechanistic fit will have the *worst* availability, and
  the agents that are easily obtained will mostly target M5/M7 rather than M1/M2 — i.e.
  **availability and mechanistic relevance will be inversely related**, which is the honest
  answer to "easily achievable."
- **P22** — Plain **water intake** will have genuine randomised human evidence on the M7 axis
  (copeptin), making it the only zero-cost, zero-risk entry in the register.
- **P23** — Nothing in the register will have been tested against **adiposity** as an outcome.
  Every trial will use limb volume, symptoms, or a biomarker. So the register will answer
  "can this mechanism be moved?" and not "does moving it reduce fat?"

**P23 is the one that matters.** If it holds, the register is a list of mechanism probes, not
of treatments for the thing this project is about, and it must be labelled that way.

### The governing finding

**Not one agent has been tested against adiposity.** Every trial used limb volume, skin
thickness, bioimpedance, symptoms, or a biomarker. The register answers "can the mechanism be
moved?" and not "does moving it change fat." Everything in it is a **mechanism probe**.

### Most informative single result

**Ubenimex.** The field's cleanest mechanistic hypothesis — LTB4 drives lymphatic dysfunction,
so inhibit LTA4H — was properly tested in humans and failed, while the *less* specific agent
sharing its pathway rationale (ketoprofen) succeeded on a surrogate. That ordering should
discipline confidence in the whole class, and it is a live argument against over-reading A1.

### Audit

- **Unresolved, recorded rather than omitted:** a search result referenced a 90-woman Royal
  Marsden lymphoedema trial that would bear on selenium. **I could not verify its intervention
  or result in either direction**, so B1 stands with that gap stated.
- **Open items:** pentoxifylline + vitamin E for post-radiation arm lymphoedema (NCT00022204),
  result not retrieved; benfotiamine, no randomised polyol-pathway trial retrieved.
- **Conflation found in my own register:** B1 mixes two different claims — volume reduction
  and erysipelas prevention. The second may be **mechanism-preserving** (infection destroys
  lymphatics) rather than mechanism-reversing. Separate them before building on either.
- **Unexpected:** that the *approved* aldose reductase inhibitor exists and is in routine use
  in three countries, while the class failed regulatory approval elsewhere — the availability
  asymmetry is geographic, not scientific.
