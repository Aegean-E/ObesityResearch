# Session 2 — Does lymphatic clearance set adipose lipid flux? And C-02

**Questions:** the gap owned at the end of session 1 (does lymphatic clearance capacity set
net adipose lipid flux?) and **C-02** (the adipocyte-size contradiction).
**Governed by** [guide.md](../guide.md) · [H1](../hypotheses/H1-water-retention-lipogenesis.md) · [claims.md](../claims.md)

---

## 1. Predictions — written before any search (guide R11)

- **P12** — Adipose microdialysis is a well-established technique with a large literature on
  interstitial glycerol, but **essentially nothing in lymphoedematous tissue**. The method
  exists, the question has not been put.
- **P13** — **C-02 resolves to methodology, not biology**: sampling method (liposuction
  aspirate vs needle biopsy select different adipocyte populations), disease stage, and
  anatomical site. Not a genuine disagreement about what lymphoedema does.
- **P14** — Lipoedema will show adipocyte **hypertrophy** more consistently than lymphoedema,
  and will have been morphometrically better studied, because the excess fat is the defining
  feature rather than an end-stage complication.
- **P15** — "Stasis" hides **stage-dependent opposite states**: lymph flow *increased* early
  (compensatory, pre-clinical) and decreased late. If so, pooling stages would produce
  exactly the kind of contradiction seen in C-02, and "lymphatic fluid stasis" as a single
  exposure is too coarse.
- **P16 — the decisive one.** ★ If **fatty acids leave adipose tissue overwhelmingly by the
  venous route** (albumin-bound into capillary blood) rather than by lymph, then lymphatic
  failure would **not** trap NEFA, and the clearance-failure premise behind §3A.1 collapses
  for a second, independent reason — not merely contradicted in direction (session 1) but
  anatomically impossible. I expect the venous route to dominate heavily. **This prediction,
  if confirmed, should end §3A.1 rather than downgrade it further.**
- **P17** — Lymph FFA will turn out to originate mainly from **plasma filtrate and
  chylomicron/lipoprotein processing**, not from local adipocyte lipolysis — meaning
  lymph-borne FFA (the adipogenic agent found in session 1) is **delivered to** the tissue
  rather than **produced by** it. That would invert the directionality of the whole picture:
  lymph is a lipid *source*, not a lipid *drain*.

**P16 and P17 together are the test of whether the lymph story is about clearance at all.**
If both hold, the mechanism is delivery, not retention — and the word "clearance" has been
misleading me since §3A.1.

## 2. Findings

### 2.0 Headline — §3A.1 is dead, and the size rule replaces it

**The lymphatic route is size-gated, and fatty acids are too small to use it.** In human
adipose tissue in vivo, the fraction of a molecule leaving by lymph rather than capillary is
**a direct function of molecular radius: 14% at 1.18 nm (IL-8) rising to 100% at 3.24 nm
(TNFα)** (n=12 healthy men, afferent peripheral lymph vs venous plasma).

NEFA (~0.4 nm, lipophilic, crossing endothelium freely) and glycerol (92 Da) sit far below
that gate. They leave by **capillary blood**. The supporting numbers:

| | Lymph | Plasma |
|---|---|---|
| NEFA | **101 ± 19 µmol/L** | **213 ± 33 µmol/L** |
| NEFA:albumin molar ratio | 0.40 ± 0.08 | 0.32 ± 0.05 (**p=0.35, NS**) |

Lymph is **NEFA-poor relative to plasma**, and its NEFA tracks its albumin at the same ratio
as plasma — i.e. the NEFA in lymph is **what came along with filtered albumin**, not a
separate export stream from adipocytes. Add the flow term — abdominal adipose blood flow
~3–5 mL·100 g⁻¹·min⁻¹ fasting, leg ~1.0 ± 0.3, against lymph flow orders of magnitude lower
— and the lymphatic contribution to fatty-acid removal is negligible on both concentration
and flow.

> **§3A.1 does not survive this.** Session 1 contradicted its *direction*; this contradicts
> its *anatomy*. Lymphatic failure cannot trap NEFA or glycerol **because they never used
> the lymphatic route**. Mark it dead, not downgraded. **P16 confirmed in substance** —
> though I had the reason wrong: it is not that lymph carries little, it is that lymph
> carries only the **large** things.

### 2.1 ★ What lymphatic failure actually retains — and it reframes the mechanism

The same rule that kills §3A.1 hands back something better. If transport is size-gated at
roughly 1–3 nm, then obstructing lymph retains **exactly the large molecules**: adipokines
and cytokines. In the same study, **all adipokines except adiponectin were more
concentrated in lymph than plasma**, with leptin and MCP-1 secreted at the highest rates
(43 µg/h ≈ 2.7 nmol/h and 32 µg/h ≈ 2.4 nmol/h).

> **Reformulation (my inference, not shown):** lymphatic stasis in adipose tissue should
> cause local retention of **adipokines and cytokines — not lipids**. TNFα (3.24 nm) is
> 100% lymph-dependent and would be fully retained; IL-8 (1.18 nm) is 86% capillary and
> would barely accumulate. So the predicted consequence of lymphatic failure is a **local
> inflammatory/adipokine milieu**, size-filtered, with lipid handling untouched.

This fits what is actually observed and previously looked incidental: the marked
mononuclear/macrophage infiltrate in the mouse tail stasis model, and the inflammatory
cytokine secretion and activated CD4⁺ T cells in human lymphoedema adipose tissue
(session 1). Those were background; on this reading they are the mechanism.

**It is also sharply testable**, because the rule is quantitative: retention in
lymphoedematous tissue should scale with molecular radius, and the ranking is predicted in
advance. A multiplex panel across a radius range, affected vs unaffected, tests it in one
experiment. **Obesity itself reduces adipose lymphatic drainage of macromolecules**, which
is the same prediction in the non-oedematous case.

### 2.2 ✗ The "~3× FFA" result from session 1 is probably a sampling artefact

Session 1 recorded that lymphoedema-derived fluid carried ~3× the free fatty acid of serum
([claims.md A3](../claims.md)). Against §2.0 that now looks wrong, and the method says why:

That fluid was obtained by **centrifuging liposuction aspirate at 300 g** and freezing at
−80 °C. Liposuction ruptures adipocytes; freeze–thaw lyses more. **A fat-rich tissue
homogenate will release free fatty acid regardless of its interstitial concentration.**
Normal adipose lymph is NEFA-*poor* relative to plasma (§2.0), so a 3× *enrichment* over
serum in the same tissue type is the opposite of the physiological baseline and more
plausibly reflects the preparation.

**Confirmed as a known pitfall by how careful groups avoid it:** the lipoedema staging study
took biopsies **by surgical dissection before infiltration of tumescent solution**, *en
bloc*, specifically to sample tissue the liposuction process had not touched.

→ **Claim A3 is downgraded to "method-confounded."** The adipogenic effect of the fluid
(A2) may still be real, but the attribution of that effect to its FFA content rests on a
measurement that the sampling method could have manufactured. The mouse lymph work (A4, A5)
is unaffected — that used cannulated lymph.

### 2.3 ✓ C-02 resolved: adipocyte hypertrophy is stage-dependent

Lipoedema, with proper morphometry (n: stage I = 9, II = 16, III = 5, controls = 14):

| Stage | Adipocyte size vs control | Fibrosis |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **No significant difference** | **Already significantly elevated** in thigh (p<0.05) |
| **II** | Significant shift to larger (p<0.05) | Further increase (p<0.005) |
| **III** | Larger, ~6,000 µm² affected thigh | Trend toward control |

Fibrosis confined to affected extremities, absent abdominally. M2-like macrophages elevated
by stage II; TNF and IL6 up in stages II–III thigh, minimal in stage I.

**This explains C-02 without either human lymphoedema study being wrong.** If hypertrophy
only appears at stage II+, then two studies sampling different stage mixes will disagree in
exactly the way observed — and the one reporting no hypertrophy may simply have had earlier
disease. **P13 and P14 confirmed; P15 supported in the matrix rather than the flow sense.**

**The more interesting finding is the ordering: fibrosis precedes hypertrophy.** Matrix
change is present at stage I with normal adipocytes. That is the opposite of the usual
telling, where adipocytes enlarge and fibrosis follows. It supports §3A.3 (tissue mechanics
as permissive) and weakens the hypoxia-from-hypertrophy sequence of §3A.2 — **and it means
the tension I flagged between §3A.2 and §3A.3 resolves in favour of mechanics coming first.**

### 2.4 P12 — microdialysis: technique abundant, question unasked

Adipose microdialysis is well established, with standard protocols, interstitial glycerol as
the lipolysis index (glycerol is not reutilised by adipose tissue), flow-rate conventions
(0.3 µL/min for true interstitial concentrations), and multicompartmental models of adipose
glycerol kinetics and capillary permeability in lean and obese humans. **Nothing located
applying it to lymphoedematous tissue.** P12 confirmed — but §2.0 has made the experiment I
proposed much less interesting, since it was designed to test a mechanism that is now dead.

## 4. Audit

**What died:** §3A.1, on anatomical grounds. Two independent refutations now (direction in
session 1, route in session 2). It should not be revisited without new anatomy.

**What was wrong in my reasoning even where the prediction was right:** P16. I expected lymph
to be a minor route in general; it is in fact the *dominant* route for anything above ~3 nm.
Getting the right answer for the wrong reason would have left me unable to see §2.1 — the
reframing only appears once the rule is understood as a **size filter** rather than a
small leak. Worth remembering: a confirmed prediction can still conceal a wrong model.

**What I should be suspicious of in my own session 1 work:** I recorded A3 (~3× FFA) without
asking how the fluid was obtained. The method was in the paper. **I read the result and not
the preparation** — a specific, repeatable failure, and the guide's R14 ("figure level")
should be read as including the methods section, not just the figures.

**Unexpected:** that fibrosis precedes adipocyte hypertrophy (§2.3). And that the adipokine
size rule exists at all, quantitatively, in humans in vivo — it is a much sharper tool than
anything else encountered so far, and it makes predictions by molecular radius.

**Where this leaves H1:** the water-centric hypothesis is now refuted on its lipid-clearance
limb as well as its lipolysis limb. What remains viable is **lymphatic stasis → size-filtered
retention of adipokines/cytokines → local inflammation → adipogenesis**, in which water is
neither agent nor vehicle but merely the thing that makes the obstruction visible.
