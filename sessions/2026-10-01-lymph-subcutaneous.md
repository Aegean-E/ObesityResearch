# Session 2026-10-01 — Lymph, subcutaneous interstitium, and H1

**Question:** Q1 (does the lymphoedematous limb carry more *fat*?), Q2 (water or lymph?),
plus how lymph interacts with osmolality, osmolarity, adipogenesis, lipogenesis.
**Predictions:** P8–P11, written last session before any search (guide R11). Scored in §1.
**Governed by** [guide.md](../guide.md) · hypothesis [H1](../hypotheses/H1-water-retention-lipogenesis.md)

---

## 0. Headline

**H1 as stated is not supported, and is contradicted on its second half.** In the one human
study that measured it, lipolysis in lymphoedematous adipose tissue is **higher**, not
suppressed, and re-esterification is **impaired**, not enhanced — the opposite of my §3A.1
mechanism.

**But a reformulated hypothesis survives and is well supported:**

> Impaired lymphatic clearance exposes adipose progenitors to **lymph-borne free fatty
> acids**, which drive adipogenesis. The water is the **vehicle, not the agent.**

So the subcutaneous instinct was right about the tissue and the pathology, and wrong about
the causal substance. "Holding water" is a misdescription; "failing to clear lymph" is the
mechanism. §3A.5's alternative reading won.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 ★ The fluid is adipogenic — and the cells are normal

The central result, from the human lymphoedema study (n=11 unilateral breast-cancer-related
lymphoedema, n=11 healthy, n=11 cancer survivors without lymphoedema):

- Adipose stromal cells **isolated from** lymphoedematous tissue were **indistinguishable**
  from healthy cells on every axis tested: population doubling time at passages 3 and 10,
  neutral lipid accumulation across **four** differentiation protocols, mRNA of PPARγ,
  perilipin, FAS, DGAT2, ATGL, insulin-stimulated Akt phosphorylation, and basal and
  stimulated lipolysis. Authors' conclusion: adipose accumulation "cannot be explained by
  higher numbers of mesenchymal stem cells or better proliferative, adipogenic or metabolic
  potential."
- But **lymphoedema-tissue-derived fluid** improved adipogenic conversion of preadipocytes
  **compared with serum**, with higher PPARγ and perilipin. Fluid obtained by centrifuging
  liposuction aspirate at 300 g, n = 6.

**This is the cleanest statement of H1b′ available:** the pathology is in the
**extracellular environment**, not in the cells. D7/E1 — human primary cells exposed to
**actual human interstitial fluid**, which is unusually good exposure realism for this
project and should be noted as a model worth imitating (guide R7, §4).

### 2.2 ★★ The agent is a lipid, not water

Two independent lines identify the adipogenic component, and both say fatty acid:

- Lymphoedema-derived fluid contained **~3× higher free fatty acids than serum**, with a
  distinct lipidome (phosphatidylserines elevated) and several-fold higher miRNA.
- In the mouse lymphatic work, lymph added to cultured preadipocytes promotes
  differentiation, and the active fraction is **lipid**: oleic, α-linolenic, palmitoleic and
  palmitic acids each induce adipogenesis in vitro. Decisively, **lymph from wild-type and
  Prox1⁺/⁻ mice did not differ in composition** — so the adipogenic property belongs to
  *normal* lymph, and the pathology is **exposure**, not altered content.

Several of these are PPARγ ligands, so the mechanism is plausibly ligand/substrate delivery
→ PPARγ → adipogenesis. **Nothing here requires water to do anything except carry the
lipid.**

**Consequence for the project:** this is not a win for H1 as posed. It is a win for the
rival reading I wrote into §3A.5, and it demotes "holding water" from mechanism to vehicle.

### 2.3 ✗ Lipolysis is UP, and re-esterification is DOWN — §3A.1 contradicted

My §3A.1 said: stagnant interstitium traps glycerol and NEFA → re-esterification → net
storage with lipolytic machinery untouched. The human data goes the other way on both counts.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Glycerol and FFA release, ex vivo explants | **Higher** in lymphoedema, basal **and** isoproterenol-stimulated (p<0.05, p<0.001) |
| Basal **FFA:glycerol ratio** | "Dramatically elevated" → **impaired re-esterification** |
| ATGL mRNA | **Lower** in diseased limb (a paradox the authors flag, against higher measured lipolysis) |
| PLIN1 mRNA | Higher |
| Isolated adipocytes, n=6–7 | Same direction; fold-change NS |

Since glycerol release is the standard index of total lipolysis (adipocytes lack glycerol
kinase, so glycerol escapes quantitatively) and FFA release is lipolysis *minus*
re-esterification, an **elevated** FFA:glycerol ratio means **less** re-esterification.
My mechanism predicted more.

**Partial defence, stated without pretending it rescues the mechanism:** these are *ex vivo*
measurements, and explant incubation washes products into the medium — which removes exactly
the in vivo clearance failure §3A.1 is about. So this measures the tissue's *intrinsic*
re-esterification capacity, not in vivo futile cycling. But that intrinsic capacity being
**lower** still argues against re-esterification as the route to storage. **§3A.1 is
downgraded, not salvaged.** The honest reading: the hypothesis's "inhibits lipolysis" half
has no support in the one human setting where it has been looked for, and some evidence
against.

### 2.4 ✗ Adipocytes are NOT larger — and the literature contradicts itself

- Mean adipocyte diameter **88.8 ± 5.6 µm** (lymphoedema) vs **97.4 ± 3.3 µm** (healthy) —
  **not significant**, and numerically the wrong way. The frequency distribution showed a
  **higher** proportion of large (100–120 µm) adipocytes in **healthy** tissue. The authors
  state their result "contradicted prior histological findings of hypertrophy."
- A separate paper reports the opposite in its title: adipocytes *are* larger in
  lymphoedematous extremities.
- The mouse tail stasis model reports fat deposition via **both** hypertrophy **and**
  increased adipocyte number, with ~2-fold increased fat thickness and raised adipogenic
  markers.

**Recorded as a contradiction, not resolved:**
> **C-02 — Adipocyte size in human lymphoedema.** Directly conflicting human reports.
> Candidate explanations: sampling site, disease stage, liposuction vs needle biopsy
> (different size selection), and whether the comparator is the contralateral limb or a
> separate person. Must be settled before any claim about hypertrophy vs hyperplasia.

This is P11 landing. "Well known clinically" turned out to mean "contested on measurement."

### 2.5 ✗✗ My T0 design has a flaw — the contralateral limb is not a clean control

Last session I wrote that the within-subject contralateral limb "cannot be argued with."
**That was wrong**, and the authors of the human study say why: the "healthy" arm of
lymphoedema patients **also shows signs of lymphatic dysfunction**. In breast-cancer-related
lymphoedema the contralateral side is not unaffected — which is unsurprising once stated,
given bilateral nodal and systemic involvement, but I asserted the opposite.

They also note paired limb samples were unavailable for most analyses on ethical grounds,
and that sampling differed between limbs (liposuction on the affected arm, needle biopsy on
the other) — a methodological asymmetry that could by itself produce an adipocyte-size
difference in either direction.

**So T0 needs rebuilding:** primary or non-oncological unilateral lymphoedema, matched
sampling method on both sides, or a different control entirely. The design is still the best
available; my confidence in it was unearned.

### 2.6 Causal direction is genuinely bidirectional — R9 bites hard

- **Lymphatic failure → fat:** *Prox1* haploinsufficiency causes **adult-onset obesity**
  via lymph leakage from mispatterned, ruptured vessels, with fat accumulating around lymph
  nodes and in mesentery. **And restoring lymphatic function rescues the obesity** — a
  reversal experiment, which is the strongest design in this whole area (guide Z5-type
  evidence). A human GWAS SNP near *PROX1* (rs1704198) associates with waist circumference.
- **Fat → lymphatic failure:** obesity impairs lymphatic function by mechanical compression,
  inflammation, and nodal fibrosis; obesity-induced lymphoedema becomes near-universal above
  BMI ~60.

So the human cross-sectional data **cannot** establish direction, and the causal weight rests
almost entirely on the mouse genetics and the rescue. Any future human claim here has to
answer R9 explicitly.

## 3. How lymph interacts with the four title terms

Answering the second half of the question directly.

### 3.1 Lymph × osmolality / osmolarity — **the link is absent, and that is the finding**

**Lymph leaving the skin is isosmotic to plasma — even after high sodium intake.** Skin-
draining lymph osmolality and Na⁺ concentration were similar on high-salt and low-salt diets.

This matters more than it first appears:

- **Lymph carries no osmotic signal.** The lymphatic route and the osmolality route are
  **separate channels**, not one mechanism. My §3A.6 convergence — in which they meet in the
  subcutaneous interstitium — is weaker than I presented it.
- Lymph protein is ~50% of plasma in skin and muscle (lymph:plasma ratios ~0.71 tail lymph,
  0.62 wick fluid, 0.50 tendon), while **electrolyte concentration is essentially identical
  to plasma**. So lymph differs from plasma in protein and lipid, **not** in tonicity.
- **The Titze hypertonic-skin model is itself contested, on exactly this ground.** Skin
  sodium can reach 180–190 mmol/L bound to glycosaminoglycans without matching water, but
  the critique is that inferring osmotically inactive storage from a raised (Na+K)/H₂O ratio
  ignores other osmolytes — and that isosmotic skin lymph argues against sustained
  interstitial hypertonicity.

**Downgrade §3A.6 accordingly.** NFAT5 → VEGF-C → lymphangiogenesis is real (below), but the
"locally hypertonic interstitium" premise it rests on is disputed, not established.

### 3.2 Lymph × sodium — confirmed pathway, but it runs the wrong way for us

- **Confirmed:** high dietary salt → local skin tonicity → macrophages act as **mobile
  osmosensors**, chemotaxing toward high-salt sites → **TonEBP/NFAT5** → **VEGF-C** →
  **skin lymphangiogenesis**, increasing lymph-capillary density and clearing sodium.
- **But the functional consequence is the opposite of stasis:** high-salt diet produced a
  **+26% increase in lymph flow** in skin, increased muscle lymph flow by PET/CT, expansion
  of the lymphatic network, and enhanced collecting-vessel contraction frequency — all
  macrophage-dependent (abolished by clodronate depletion).

> **C-03 — The salt arrow points away from adipogenesis.** If lymph **stasis** is what
> drives adipose accumulation (§2.1–2.2), and high salt **increases** lymph flow and
> lymphatic density, then high salt should be **anti**-adipogenic through this route. That
> is in direct tension with the sodium–adiposity association in
> [guide.md §2.6](../guide.md). Either the sodium–adiposity link runs through a different
> mechanism entirely (palatability remains the leading candidate), or the lymphatic response
> is a *compensation* that fails at some point, or one of the two observations is wrong.

Counter-evidence also found, per R12: engineered skin lymphangiogenesis **did not** affect
blood pressure in mouse salt-sensitive hypertension models — a null against the functional
importance of this axis.

### 3.3 Lymph × adipogenesis — the strongest link in the session

Established well enough to build on: lymph is adipogenic to preadipocytes, the agent is its
free fatty acids, normal lymph suffices, and the pathology is ectopic exposure (§2.2).
Supported by genetic cause (*Prox1*), reversal (lymphatic restoration), a stasis model
(mouse tail, both hyperplasia and hypertrophy), and human fluid-on-cells data.

### 3.4 Lymph × lipogenesis — **a real gap**

- Lymph is the obligatory route for **dietary** lipid (chyle, chylomicrons), so the lymphatic
  system is a lipid *transport* system by construction.
- **But:** DNL was **not measured** in the human lymphoedema study; lipogenic genes moved
  inconsistently (ChREBP ↓, ELOVL6 ↓, FASN ↑ paired, overall "not substantially changed");
  and the review literature on lymphatics and adipose metabolism **does not address**
  lipolysis rates or fatty-acid clearance *from* adipose tissue at all.
- So the lymphatic system's role in **removing** lipolysis products — the premise of
  §3A.1 — is close to unstudied, independently of §2.3 contradicting its predicted direction.

**This is the gap worth owning.** Not "is lymph adipogenic" (answered, yes, via FFA) but
**"does lymphatic clearance capacity set net adipose lipid flux?"** Adipose microdialysis
plus lymph flow measurement could address it, and I found nothing that has.

## 4. Audit (guide §5 step 6)

**Predictions wrong:** none outright wrong this session; P8, P9, P11 confirmed. But P11 was
a prediction *that I would err*, and it was right — the error it caught was real (§2.4).

**My claims that weakened:**
1. **§3A.1 clearance→re-esterification→storage** — contradicted in direction by human data
   (§2.3). Downgraded.
2. **§3A.4 contralateral limb as an unarguable control** — wrong (§2.5). Corrected.
3. **§3A.6 the NFAT5 convergence** — premise (interstitial hypertonicity) is contested, and
   the salt→lymph-flow direction opposes the story (§3.1, §3.2). Downgraded.
4. **H1's "inhibits lipolysis" half** — unsupported, with evidence against.

**Counter-evidence found (R12):** higher not lower lipolysis; impaired not enhanced
re-esterification; no adipocyte hypertrophy; lipogenic genes unmoved; obesity→lymphatic
dysfunction as a competing direction; salt increasing rather than stalling lymph flow;
null lymphangiogenesis/BP result.

**What was unexpected:** that the adipogenic agent in lymph is *identified*, and is a
**fatty acid**. I expected the field to be vague about the active component; instead the
answer is specific and it dissolves the water-centric framing. Also unexpected: that
isolated lymphoedema cells are *completely normal* — a strong, clean result pointing all
causal weight at the extracellular milieu.

**Status of the four terms:** osmolality/osmolarity turn out to be **disconnected** from the
lymphatic route (lymph is isosmotic). Adipogenesis is **connected**, via lymph FFA.
Lipogenesis is **unmeasured**. That is a sharper map than the session started with, and it
narrows the project considerably.
