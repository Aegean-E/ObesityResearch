# G01 — Fructose-specific craving experienced as necessity

**Recorded** 2026-10-02 · **Status:** open, hypothesis-generating only
**Evidence code: D8/E1** — n-of-1 self-observation. Under
[guide.md](../guide.md) this can **start** a mechanism search and direct the literature. It
**cannot support a claim**, and must never be presented in the same breath as a measured
finding as though they carried equal weight.

---

## 1. The observation, in the subject's own words

> "Sometimes my body craves fructose, not glucose — not as pleasure but as necessity, just
> like low blood glucose."

Quoted unaltered. Three claims are embedded, and they are separable — which matters, because
they have different mechanisms and different tests:

| | Claim | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | The craving is **substrate-specific**: fructose, not glucose | If true, this is the whole point. A generic sugar craving has no mechanism worth chasing |
| **C2** | It is **not hedonic** — not wanting a pleasant taste | Rules out the sweet-taste receptor and reward pathways; implies a **post-ingestive**, visceral or hepatic signal |
| **C3** | It is **urgent/homeostatic**, phenomenologically like hypoglycaemia | Implies a sensed deficit with a defended set point, not a preference |

**C1 is the one at risk.** Fructose rarely arrives alone: fruit carries water, potassium,
fibre, sorbitol and organic acids; honey carries glucose in near-equal measure. A craving
satisfied by fruit is not evidence of a fructose requirement. **Testing C1 needs pure
fructose versus pure dextrose, matched for sweetness and ideally blinded** — see §4.

## 2. Predictions, before searching (guide R11)

- **P30** — There is a metabolic state in which **fructose is usable and glucose is not**, and
  it will turn on fructose entering glycolysis **below phosphofructokinase-1**. Glucose depends
  on PFK-1; fructose does not.
- **P31** — A named human disorder will exist in which **glucose fails to relieve, or worsens,**
  an exertional energy-failure state — and it will be a **PFK-pathway** defect rather than a
  phosphorylase one. If so, the subject's phenomenology has a precedent in the clinical
  literature rather than being idiosyncratic.
- **P32** — **Small** doses of fructose will be documented to *improve* human glucose handling
  (a "catalytic" effect), mechanistically via **fructose-1-phosphate releasing glucokinase from
  its regulatory protein**. That would make fructose a **permissive signal for glucose use** —
  guide R15's "permissive" category — which would explain a craving for fructose arising
  precisely when glucose is abundant but unusable.
- **P33** — Fructose will be documented to replenish **hepatic** glycogen preferentially over
  muscle, more efficiently than glucose alone.
- **P34** — The known gut→brain post-ingestive sugar-preference circuit will be **glucose-
  preferring and SGLT1-dependent**, i.e. it will **not** explain a fructose-specific craving.
  So C2 would need a different afferent, and the mechanism would be hepatic rather than
  intestinal.

**P32 is the one I expect to be most informative**, and P34 the one most likely to force the
explanation somewhere unexpected.

## 3. Findings

### 3.0 Headline — everything fructose-specific is hepatic, so the observation points at the liver

Four predictions, four confirmed, and together they converge on one organ. **Every mechanism
by which fructose differs from glucose is a liver mechanism**, and the one circuit that makes
mammals crave sugar post-ingestively is **blind to fructose**. If C1 and C2 both hold — the
craving is fructose-specific *and* not hedonic — then the signal is **hepatic, not
intestinal and not gustatory**. That is a real narrowing from a single sentence of
phenomenology.

### 3.1 ★★ P32 confirmed — fructose is a *permissive key* for hepatic glucose uptake

This is the strongest finding and it maps onto the observation almost too neatly.

**Mechanism, molecularly specific:** **fructose-1-phosphate inhibits binding of glucokinase to
glucokinase regulatory protein** (while fructose-6-phosphate *enhances* it). GKRP is
**liver-specific** and holds glucokinase sequestered in the nucleus. F1P releases it into the
cytosol, where it phosphorylates glucose — the rate-determining step of hepatic glucose
metabolism.

**Human evidence:** small "catalytic" fructose doses given with glucose **reduce the glycaemic
response to a glucose load**, and the effect is largest **in those with the poorest glucose
tolerance**. Documented in healthy adults and in type 2 diabetes. Catalytic fructose increases
hepatic glucose uptake and glycogen storage, improves glucose tolerance, and **restores the
ability of blood glucose to suppress gluconeogenesis**.

> **Why this fits the observation.** In a state where glucokinase is GKRP-sequestered, blood
> glucose is **present but hepatically unusable**, and **fructose-1-phosphate is the only
> signal that unlocks it.** Craving fructose while glucose fails to satisfy is exactly what a
> deficit of the permissive signal would feel like — guide R15's **"permissive"** category,
> arrived at from the subject's side rather than the literature's.
>
> **Sharp, cheap, falsifiable prediction:** the craving should be satisfied by a **small**
> amount of fructose — catalytic doses in these trials are on the order of grams, not a large
> fruit load — and should then **restore the usefulness of glucose**. A requirement for large
> amounts argues against this mechanism and for §3.3.

[Diabetes Care 2001, acute fructose in T2D](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/24/11/1882/24748/Acute-Fructose-Administration-Improves-Oral) ·
[FACE trial](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6024645/) ·
[hepatic glucose metabolism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12806219/)

### 3.2 ★ An unexpected lead directly on the phenomenology

The subject compared the sensation to hypoglycaemia. There is a paper titled
**"Fructose Normalizes Specific Counterregulatory Responses to Hypoglycemia in Patients With
Type 1 Diabetes"** (Diabetes 2005). I have **not** read it at figure level — recorded as a
**lead, not a finding** — but a documented interaction between fructose and the
counterregulatory response to hypoglycaemia is a close enough hit on "just like low blood
glucose" that it should be the next thing read.
[Diabetes 2005](https://diabetesjournals.org/diabetes/article/54/3/609/14909/Fructose-Normalizes-Specific-Counterregulatory)

### 3.3 ✅ P33 confirmed with numbers — fructose doubles hepatic glycogen repletion

| Condition | Liver glycogen synthesis |
|---|---|
| Glucose + fructose | **8.1 ± 0.6 g·h⁻¹** |
| Glucose alone | **3.7 ± 0.5 g·h⁻¹** |

By ¹³C magnetic resonance spectroscopy in humans; typical repletion ~7.3 g·h⁻¹ with
glucose+fructose, roughly **double** glucose alone. Clearest when carbohydrate intake exceeds
**0.9 g·kg⁻¹·h⁻¹**. Two reasons: separate intestinal transporters (GLUT5 vs SGLT1) raise total
absorption, and fructose is **preferentially metabolised by the liver**.

**Alternative reading of the observation:** if the sensed deficit is **hepatic glycogen**,
fructose is the correct nutrient and glucose is a poor substitute — and this one predicts
**larger** doses and a link to exertion or fasting, distinguishing it from §3.1.
[J Physiol 2019](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1113/JP277116) ·
[post-exercise recovery](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5409683/)

### 3.4 ✅✅ P34 confirmed — the sugar-craving circuit cannot see fructose

Duodenal **neuropod cells** detect glucose via **SGLT1**, release glutamate onto vagal
afferents, and drive the **caudal nucleus of the solitary tract**, basal-ganglia reward
circuits and hypothalamic feeding circuits. This is the pathway that generates post-ingestive
sugar preference.

**It is selective for glucose. SGLT1 is not activated by fructose** (which uses GLUT5), and
**intragastric fructose neither stimulated intake nor conditioned a flavour preference.**
Silencing the circuit abolishes learned sugar preference.

> **This is the most useful negative in the record.** The known machinery for wanting sugar
> *is blind to fructose*. So a genuinely fructose-specific craving **cannot** be that circuit —
> which forces the explanation **upstream into the liver**, and independently corroborates
> §3.1 and §3.3 over any gut-based account.

[Tan et al., Nature 2020](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e8c5d989638d05da5c49dd4/t/5e972ebf887d660b99ac5f2e/1586966247022/2020+Nature+Tan.pdf) ·
[neural basis of sugar preference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9886228/) ·
[gut sensor cell](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-021-00982-7)

### 3.5 ◐ P30/P31 — right about the biochemistry, wrong about where it applies

**Confirmed:** fructose enters glycolysis below PFK-1, and a human disorder exists with
precisely the phenomenology of glucose being unusable — **GSD VII (Tarui, muscle PFKM
deficiency)**: exercise intolerance, myalgia, cramps, hyperuricaemia, compensated haemolysis,
"inability to use glucose as an energy resource," and distinctively **no second wind**, unlike
McArdle disease. Management is dietary — high protein, or low-carbohydrate/high-fat, with a
ketogenic diet reported beneficial.

**But my own reasoning had a hole, and it matters:** the PFK bypass is **hepatic only**.
Muscle phosphorylates fructose with **hexokinase to fructose-6-phosphate — which is *above*
PFK-1** — so fructose does **not** bypass the block in muscle. Only the liver has the
KHK→aldolase B route to triose phosphates. **So fructose would not rescue muscle PFK
deficiency**, and P30's logic survives only for the liver.

**Not confirmed:** I expected to find that glucose actively *worsens* GSD VII exertion. The
sources confirm absent second wind and glucose being unusable; they do **not** state glucose
worsens it. **Recorded as unverified rather than asserted.**

This correction is itself the useful part: it removes muscle from the picture and leaves the
liver, consistent with §3.0–3.4.
[Ketogenic diet in GSD VII](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7010930/) ·
[overview](https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/2500183-overview)

## 4. The test that would make C1 real

C1 (substrate specificity) is the load-bearing claim and currently rests on introspection
against confounded foods: fruit carries water, potassium, fibre, sorbitol and acids; honey
carries nearly as much glucose as fructose. **A craving relieved by fruit is not evidence of a
fructose requirement.**

**Design, stated so it cannot drift (guide R13):**

- **Comparison:** pure crystalline **fructose** vs pure **dextrose**, matched for sweetness
  (fructose is sweeter, so dextrose needs a non-nutritive sweetener to match, or the comparison
  is unblinded by taste alone).
- **Primary outcome, pre-specified:** time to relief of the sensation, recorded before knowing
  which was taken.
- **Blinding:** opaque, pre-coded sachets prepared by someone else. Self-blinding fails here,
  because fructose and dextrose taste different.
- **Dose, as the discriminator:** a **small** dose (grams) tests §3.1 (catalytic/GKRP); a
  **larger** dose tests §3.3 (hepatic glycogen). Running both separates the two hypotheses,
  which is the whole point.
- **Repetition:** multiple episodes, order randomised. A single episode is an anecdote about an
  anecdote.
- **Context recorded each time:** hours fasted, exertion, sleep, time of day.

This is a genuine n-of-1 crossover and would raise C1 from **D8** to something worth citing.

## 5. Where this stops being a research question

Stated plainly rather than buried, because the distinction is real. This project maps
mechanism; it does not interpret a person's symptoms. A recurrent craving experienced as
**urgent necessity** rather than appetite is a symptom, and the phenomenology above overlaps
with named metabolic entities (the glycogen storage and glycolytic-enzyme disorders among
them) that are diagnosed with measurements — not with a literature map and not by me.

So: **§3 is a mechanism map and §4 is a self-experiment about a sensation. Neither is a
workup**, and if the episodes are frequent, intense, or carry exertional myalgia, cramps,
dark urine or haemolysis alongside, that belongs with someone who can order the tests. The
subject is a neurologist and does not need telling how; the boundary is a division of labour,
not a comment on competence.

## 6. Audit

**Predictions:** P32 ✅ (strongest), P33 ✅ with numbers, P34 ✅✅ (the most useful negative),
P30/P31 ◐ — biochemistry right, **organ wrong**, and the "glucose worsens GSD VII" claim left
unverified rather than asserted.

**My own error, recorded:** I reasoned that fructose bypasses PFK-1 without checking **which
tissue** has the enzymes to do it. Muscle hexokinase puts fructose *above* the block. Had I
not checked, I would have built a muscle hypothesis on a liver mechanism — the same shape of
error as session 2's clearance mistake, where I had a correct mechanism and applied it in the
wrong compartment. **Twice now. The pattern is mine: correct biochemistry, wrong location.**
Watch for it.

**What converged:** four independent lines all put fructose's distinctiveness in the liver,
including a negative (§3.4) that rules out the obvious gut explanation. Convergence from a
negative is worth more than convergence from positives.

**Open:** §3.2 unread at figure level. The §4 experiment unrun. Whether fructose craving has
ever been described as a clinical entity in its own right — not searched.
